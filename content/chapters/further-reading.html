Further Reading and Foundational Works

This manuscript presents a personal synthesis of ideas concerning spacetime, quantum mechanics, the direction of time, free will, and time travel. Its arguments were developed through independent reasoning and extensive discussions, rather than as a systematic review of the scientific and philosophical literature.

The works listed below provide foundational scientific results, historical context, and additional perspectives on the subjects discussed. They are offered as resources for readers wishing to explore these topics further. Their inclusion does not imply that the arguments presented in this manuscript were derived directly from these publications, nor that their authors endorse the interpretations advanced here.

Relativity, Spacetime, and the Block Universe

  • Albert Einstein (1905). On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies. Foundational formulation of special relativity, including the relativity of simultaneity.
  • Hermann Minkowski (1908). Space and Time. The four-dimensional spacetime picture developed from special relativity; linked text is an English translation.
  • Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Being and Becoming in Modern Physics. An overview of the philosophical debate about time, becoming, and the implications of relativity.

Thermodynamics and the Direction of Time

  • Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Thermodynamic Asymmetry in Time. Background on entropy, statistical mechanics, and why the thermodynamic arrow has a preferred direction.

Quantum Mechanics and Bell's Theorem

  • John S. Bell (1964). On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen Paradox. The foundational inequality argument and the assumptions of local hidden-variable descriptions.
  • Michael J. W. Hall (2010). Local Deterministic Model of Singlet State Correlations Based on Relaxing Measurement Independence. An example of prior research on setting-dependent hidden-variable distributions; the paper's model is deterministic and is not the model constructed in this manuscript.
  • Yoon-Ho Kim and colleagues (2000). Delayed “Choice” Quantum Eraser. A foundational experiment involving delayed-choice arrangements and conditional detection statistics.

Free Will

  • Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Free Will. An introduction to compatibilist, incompatibilist, libertarian, and related accounts of agency.

Time Travel and Causal Consistency

  • John Friedman and colleagues (1990). Cauchy Problem in Spacetimes with Closed Timelike Curves. Research on the constraints imposed by closed timelike curves and globally consistent physical solutions.
  • Stephen W. Hawking (1992). Chronology Protection Conjecture. A proposal concerning whether the laws of physics prevent the formation of closed timelike curves.
