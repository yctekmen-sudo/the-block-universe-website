3 The Laws of Physics in a Static Block Universe

3.1 How to Think About the Laws of Physics in a Static Block Universe

Because human observers perceive time as flowing, we naturally tend to think of the laws of physics in dynamic terms. We imagine the universe as having a particular physical state at the present moment, with the laws of physics determining what happens next. In this picture, the current “now” can be thought of conceptually as an input, the next “now” as an output, and the laws of physics as the rules that relate the two:

Current state  →  laws of physics  →  next state

This is a useful conceptual model of physical evolution. It corresponds naturally to our experience of time: one state exists “now,” physical processes occur, and another state subsequently becomes the new “now.”, similar to an algorithm with inputs and outputs.

This algorithm analogy is intended only to make this conceptual distinction clear. It should not be interpreted literally. The laws of physics need not constitute an algorithm in the computational sense, nor do they necessarily imply that the universe is deterministic. An algorithm, in the conceptual sense used here, could contain a probabilistic or random component. The important point is simply that the dynamic description treats the physical state at one time as being related by the laws of physics to the physical state at another time.

The Block Universe provides another way of describing the same physical reality.

If the past, present, and future all exist as parts of a complete four-dimensional spacetime, then the universe does not need to be thought of as continually generating a new “now.” There is no physical process in which the present moment disappears and is replaced by a newly created future moment. The complete history of the universe is already represented within the four-dimensional structure.

This changes the way in which we can think about the laws of physics.

Rather than regarding the laws as rules that are continually being executed as the universe advances from one moment to the next, we can regard them as being enshrined in the self-consistent physical relationships contained within the Block Universe itself.

For example, instead of imagining a physical object as repeatedly being updated from one position to another,

x(T1) → x(T2) → x(T3)

we can regard its complete worldline as part of the four-dimensional physical structure. The laws of physics describe the relationships between the different points along that worldline and between that worldline and the other physical systems with which it interacts.

From this perspective, the laws of physics do not have to be thought of as something external to the Block Universe that is continually acting upon it. Rather, the physical contents of the block exhibit the relationships described by those laws.

Dynamic view:
The laws relate the current state to subsequent states.

Block Universe view:
The complete physical history exhibits the relationships described by the laws.

These are not necessarily competing descriptions. They are two ways of conceptualizing the same physical relationships.

The dynamic description emphasizes evolution. The Block Universe description emphasizes self-consistency.

This distinction also changes how we should think about our own experience of discovering the laws of physics. Because we experience time as flowing, we encounter the physical world one state at a time. We observe an object in one state, then observe it in another, and discover that the relationship between those states is consistent. We perform an experiment, obtain a result, repeat the experiment, and find the same physical relationship again.

In this sense, our perception of the flow of time causes us to encounter the self-consistent structure of the Block Universe sequentially. What we describe as a law of physics is, from this perspective, a relationship that remains consistently manifested throughout the relevant regions of spacetime.

We therefore do not need to imagine that the universe is repeatedly “applying” the laws of physics in real time in order for physical laws to govern what we observe. The complete spacetime structure can simply embody those relationships. As observers embedded within that structure, we encounter them sequentially and interpret their persistent consistency as the laws governing the evolution of physical systems.

This provides an important conceptual bridge between the static and dynamic descriptions of reality.

The Block Universe is static as a four-dimensional entity, while the physical relationships within it are precisely those that, when experienced along an observer’s worldline, appear as dynamical evolution.

This distinction will become particularly important when considering quantum mechanics. If the laws of physics can be understood either as governing successive physical states or as describing the self-consistent relationships contained within the complete spacetime structure, then the apparent transition from one quantum state to another must also be examined from both perspectives. In particular, it raises the question of what, within a static four-dimensional description, the conventional notion of wave-function collapse is actually describing.
