Appendix A. CHSH and the Explicit Measurement-Dependent Local Hidden-Variable Model

This appendix contains the technical development underlying the discussion in Section 4. It is intentionally separated from the main narrative so that the conceptual argument can proceed without requiring the reader to follow the full CHSH algebra. The appendix derives the CHSH bound for local hidden-variable models, identifies the precise role of measurement independence, and presents the explicit measurement-dependent local hidden-variable model used in the main text.

The appendix supplies the mathematical support for the conclusions summarized in Section 4. It does not by itself establish that the Block Universe is the physical mechanism responsible for measurement dependence; that remains an open physical question discussed in the main text.

A.1 The CHSH Experiment

The CHSH formulation makes the logical structure of Bell's theorem especially transparent. Instead of considering a single measurement setting at each location, Alice has a choice between two settings, a and a′, while Bob has a choice between two settings, b and b′. Each measurement produces an outcome of either +1 or −1.

For any pair of settings, define the correlation

E(a,b) = ⟨AB⟩.

The CHSH combination is then

S = E(a,b) + E(a,b′) + E(a′,b) − E(a′,b′).

The remarkable feature of CHSH is that a local hidden-variable model satisfying measurement independence places a strict upper bound on this quantity.

A.2 The Local Deterministic CHSH Bound

Begin with the deterministic special case:

A = A(a,λ),    B = B(b,λ),

where every outcome is either +1 or −1.

For a particular value of λ, define

S(λ) = A(a,λ)B(b,λ) + A(a,λ)B(b′,λ) + A(a′,λ)B(b,λ) − A(a′,λ)B(b′,λ).

Factor this expression:

S(λ) = A(a,λ)[B(b,λ)+B(b′,λ)] + A(a′,λ)[B(b,λ)-B(b′,λ)].

Because B(b,λ) and B(b′,λ) can each only be +1 or −1, one of the two bracketed terms must be zero and the other must be either +2 or −2. Therefore

|S(λ)| = 2.

This is an important point: the local algebra itself does not yet use measurement independence. The bound arises for each individual hidden-variable state.

A.3 Where Measurement Independence Enters CHSH

Under measurement independence, the same probability distribution ρ(λ) is used for all four setting combinations:

E(a,b) = ∫ dλ ρ(λ) A(a,λ)B(b,λ),

E(a,b′) = ∫ dλ ρ(λ) A(a,λ)B(b′,λ),

E(a′,b) = ∫ dλ ρ(λ) A(a′,λ)B(b,λ),

E(a′,b′) = ∫ dλ ρ(λ) A(a′,λ)B(b′,λ).

Because the same distribution appears in all four expressions, they can be combined:

S = ∫ dλ ρ(λ) S(λ).

Since |S(λ)| = 2 for every λ, it follows that

|S| ≤ 2.

This is the CHSH inequality.

The exact point at which measurement independence enters is therefore extremely specific. It is not the algebraic identity that gives |S(λ)|=2. It is the assumption that the four experimentally different setting combinations can all be represented as averages over the same underlying distribution ρ(λ).

If instead the distribution depends on the settings, we have

ρ(λ|a,b),   ρ(λ|a,b′),   ρ(λ|a′,b),   ρ(λ|a′,b′),

and the four terms cannot in general be combined into a single integral of S(λ). The step producing |S|≤2 is then unavailable.

Measurement independence → one common ρ(λ) → CHSH bound.

This is the mathematical opening on which the remainder of the proposal rests.

A.4 The Quantum Prediction

For a pair of spin-½ particles prepared in the singlet state, quantum mechanics predicts the correlation

EQM(a,b) = −cos(θab),

where θab is the angle between the measurement directions.

Choose, for example,

a = 0°,   a′ = 90°,   b = 45°,   b′ = −45°.

The corresponding quantum correlations give

|SQM| = 2√2.

Since

2√2 > 2,

the quantum prediction violates the CHSH bound.

The experimental significance is straightforward: measurements reproduce correlations incompatible with the assumptions used to derive the local, measurement-independent CHSH inequality.

A.5 Deterministic Notation Does Not Require Deterministic Ontology

At this stage it is important not to overinterpret the notation A(a,λ) and B(b,λ). The deterministic treatment is mathematically convenient because it makes the CHSH logic transparent. The broader proposal does not require that nature be deterministic at the hidden-variable level.

A more general local stochastic model would instead specify conditional probabilities such as

P(A|a,λ),    P(B|b,λ).

Locality can then be represented by the factorization

P(A,B|a,b,λ) = P(A|a,λ)P(B|b,λ).

Measurement dependence concerns the relationship between λ and the settings, not whether the response functions themselves are deterministic. The proposal therefore remains compatible with genuinely stochastic underlying physics.

A.6 The Constructive Question

The CHSH analysis has established the logical opening, but a logical opening is not yet a physical model. To make the proposal more substantial, we need to show that a measurement-dependent local hidden-variable construction can actually reproduce the quantum correlations.

Such constructions are known in the foundations of physics literature. A particularly simple example, associated with Brans and discussed explicitly by Michael Hall, uses a hidden variable with two binary components. The model is useful here not because it constitutes the complete physical theory being proposed, but because it demonstrates that relaxing measurement independence is mathematically sufficient to construct local response functions reproducing the singlet statistics.

A.7 The Hidden Variable

Let the hidden variable be

λ = (λ1, λ2),

where

λ1, λ2 ∈ {+1,−1}.

There are therefore four possible values of λ.

Define the local measurement responses simply as

A(x,λ) = λ1,     B(y,λ) = λ2.

These response functions are manifestly local in the relevant sense: Alice's result depends on her local hidden-variable component and not on Bob's measurement direction, while Bob's result depends on his local hidden-variable component and not on Alice's measurement direction.

A.8 The Setting-Dependent Hidden-Variable Distribution

The essential ingredient is the distribution assigned to the hidden variable. Instead of assuming a setting-independent distribution, define

ρ(λ|x,y) = [1 − λ1λ2(x·y)] / 4.

Here x and y are the two measurement directions, represented as unit vectors.

This equation contains the entire departure from measurement independence. The local response functions remain simple. All of the angular dependence required to reproduce the quantum correlation is placed in the conditional distribution of the hidden variable.

A.9 Validity of the Probability Distribution

A probability distribution must be non-negative and must sum to one.

Because λ1λ2=±1 and −1≤x·y≤1, the numerator satisfies

0 ≤ 1 − λ1λ2(x·y) ≤ 2.

Thus

ρ(λ|x,y) ≥ 0.

Summing over the four possible values of λ gives

Σλ ρ(λ|x,y) = [4 − (x·y)Σλλ1λ2]/4 = 1,

because the four values contain equal numbers with λ1λ2}=+1 and −1.

The construction is therefore a valid conditional probability distribution.

A.10 The Local Marginals

A crucial property of the model is that Alice's marginal statistics remain independent of Bob's setting, and vice versa.

Since Alice's outcome is A=λ1, sum over λ2:

P(A=+1|x,y) = 1/2,    P(A=−1|x,y) = 1/2.

Likewise,

P(B=+1|x,y) = 1/2,    P(B=−1|x,y) = 1/2.

Thus, neither observer can infer the distant measurement setting merely by examining the local outcome statistics.

This is important conceptually. The model is measurement-dependent, but the dependence does not provide an operational faster-than-light signaling channel.

A.11 Reproducing the Singlet Correlation

Because the outcomes are

A = λ1,    B = λ2,

their product is

AB = λ1λ2.

The predicted correlation is therefore

E(AB|x,y) = Σλ ρ(λ|x,y) λ1λ2.

Substituting the conditional distribution gives

E(AB|x,y) = Σλ [1 − λ1λ2(x·y)] λ1λ2/4.

The first term sums to zero because the four hidden-variable states contain equal numbers of positive and negative products. The second term gives

E(AB|x,y) = −x·y.

For unit measurement vectors this is exactly

E(AB|x,y) = −cos(θxy),

which is the singlet correlation predicted by quantum mechanics.

A.12 Reproducing the Full Singlet Joint Probabilities

The construction is stronger than reproducing only the average correlation. Since A=λ1 and B=λ2, the joint probability of outcomes a,b∈{±1} is

P(A=a,B=b|x,y) = [1 − ab(x·y)]/4.

This is precisely the singlet-state joint probability distribution for spin measurements along directions x and y.

Thus, the simple model does not merely produce the correct value of E(AB). It reproduces the complete two-outcome probability distribution for the singlet measurement.

A.13 What the Explicit Model Demonstrates

The model demonstrates something very specific and important:

Local response functions + measurement dependence → exact singlet statistics.

It therefore establishes that Bell-violating correlations do not logically require the response functions themselves to contain a dependence on the distant measurement setting.

However, the model should not be described simply as an ordinary Bell-local hidden-variable theory. Bell locality, in the full sense relevant to the theorem, includes the statistical independence assumption. Because the distribution ρ(λ|x,y) depends on the settings, the model violates measurement independence.

The most precise description is therefore a measurement-dependent local hidden-variable model, or a model with local response functions and setting-dependent hidden-variable statistics.

A.14 The Model Is Not Yet the Block Universe

The mathematical construction should not be confused with the physical interpretation proposed in this manuscript.

The equations above demonstrate that a particular conditional probability distribution can reproduce the singlet correlations while the response functions remain local. They do not, by themselves, explain why that conditional distribution has the required form.

That is where the Block Universe enters conceptually. The proposal is that the setting-dependent distribution may be understood not as a mysterious dynamical influence from a future measurement into the past, but as a property of the complete four-dimensional physical history.

The mathematical model shows that such correlations are possible. The Block Universe supplies the spacetime picture within which the existence of such correlations can be considered without automatically interpreting them as causal signals traveling backward through time.

A.15 Correlation Across Spacetime Is Not Interaction Across Spacetime

Suppose a complete physical history contains the source preparation, the hidden variable λ, Alice's eventual setting x, Bob's eventual setting y, and the corresponding outcomes.

H = {preparation, λ, x, y, A, B, measurement records, spacetime geometry, ...}

In a Block Universe description, these are not pieces of reality that must be created one after another. They are components of one four-dimensional history.

Consequently, a statistical relationship between λ and later settings does not automatically imply that the later settings physically caused λ. It may instead be a constraint on which complete histories occur with which probabilities.

Statistical dependence ≠ dynamical causation.

This distinction is central to the proposal. Without it, the rejection of measurement independence immediately appears to require retrocausal signaling. With it, the possibility of a globally correlated four-dimensional history becomes conceptually distinct from a signal propagating backward through time.

A.16 Does This Mean the Future Determines the Past?

That language is tempting but potentially misleading.

In the ordinary dynamical picture, one asks whether a later event causes an earlier event. The Block Universe picture does not require that question to have the same role. Instead, it asks whether the complete spacetime solution satisfies a set of global consistency conditions.

Consider an ordinary four-dimensional geometric object. Its distant parts can stand in exact mathematical relationships to one another without one part exerting a force on another. The relationship is a property of the whole object.

The proposal applies an analogous idea to physical histories. The correlation between a hidden variable and measurement settings is not necessarily a new causal process. It can instead be part of the structure selecting which complete physical history is realized.

This does not prove that nature actually works this way. It identifies the conceptual possibility that must be examined by a complete theory.

A.17 Ontic Randomness Does Not Require Statistical Independence

Another important distinction concerns randomness.

If λ is genuinely random, it does not follow that λ must be statistically independent of every other variable in the universe. Randomness and independence are different mathematical concepts.

A variable can have a nontrivial probability distribution while remaining correlated with another variable. In the present construction, the hidden variable is not assigned a single fixed value in advance from the perspective of the observer. Instead, different complete histories occur with different probabilities, and those probabilities depend on the measurement settings that form part of the complete history.

Thus, the proposal does not have to say that quantum randomness is merely ignorance about a pre-existing deterministic state. It can retain genuine probabilistic structure while rejecting the stronger assumption that the random variable must be statistically independent of later measurement choices.

A.18 The Entire Angular Dependence Resides in the Hidden-Variable Distribution

The explicit model makes the structure particularly transparent.

The response functions are simply

A(x,λ)=λ1,    B(y,λ)=λ2.

They contain no dependence on the distant measurement direction.

All of the angular dependence required to reproduce the quantum correlation appears in

ρ(λ|x,y) = [1 − λ1λ2(x·y)]/4.

The quantum angular correlation is therefore represented as a property of the distribution of complete histories rather than as a nonlocal modification of a local response function.

A.19 No-Signaling Is Preserved

The model's measurement dependence should not be confused with operational signaling.

Although the hidden-variable distribution depends jointly on x and y, the local marginal distributions remain

P(A=±1|x,y)=1/2,

P(B=±1|x,y)=1/2.

Therefore, Alice's local statistics do not reveal Bob's setting, and Bob's local statistics do not reveal Alice's setting.

This distinction is important for the proposed interpretation. The aim is not to introduce a hidden communication channel that transmits usable information faster than light. The aim is to account for the observed joint correlations without requiring such a channel.

A.20 The Price of the Proposal: Measurement Independence Is a Real Assumption

The measurement-dependent construction does not make Bell's theorem disappear. On the contrary, it clarifies exactly what Bell's theorem tells us.

If measurement independence is imposed, the CHSH inequality follows for the local hidden-variable framework described above. Quantum mechanics violates that inequality. Therefore, a local hidden-variable account reproducing the quantum correlations must relax at least one of the assumptions entering the Bell derivation.

The present proposal chooses to relax measurement independence.

That is a substantive physical commitment, not a mathematical trick. The question is whether the resulting commitment can be given a natural physical interpretation and developed into a complete theory without sacrificing the empirical successes of quantum mechanics.

A.21 The Central Proposal, Sharpened

The proposal developed in this section can now be stated more precisely.

A local hidden-variable model may reproduce the exact singlet correlations if the hidden-variable distribution is allowed to depend statistically on the measurement settings:

ρ(λ|x,y) ≠ ρ(λ).

The local response functions may remain of the form

A=A(x,λ),    B=B(y,λ),

with no superluminal dependence of one local outcome on the distant setting.

The Block Universe interpretation proposed here is that the setting dependence may represent a correlation among components of one complete four-dimensional physical history rather than a dynamical influence propagating from the future into the past.

A.22 The Observer Still Experiences a Sequence

None of this changes the observer's ordinary experience of time.

Along Alice's worldline, there is still a sequence of physical states:

S1 → S2 → S3.

At one state, she prepares the experiment. At a later state, she encounters a particular measurement setting and records an outcome. At still later states, she remembers that outcome and incorporates it into subsequent reasoning.

The fact that the complete history contains the eventual outcome does not mean that Alice experiences that outcome before the measurement event. Her physical state at the earlier point does not ordinarily contain a usable record of the later result.

This preserves the distinction developed earlier between the four-dimensional existence of events and the sequential information structure of an embedded observer.

A.23 What the Block Universe Does and Does Not Explain

At this point the role of the Block Universe must be kept carefully bounded.

It provides a conceptual framework in which:

  • past and future events can be components of one four-dimensional history;

  • statistical correlations need not automatically be interpreted as causal signals;

  • measurement events can be treated as local physical events within the spacetime structure;

  • the observer's sequential experience remains compatible with a complete spacetime description.

But the Block Universe does not, by itself, derive the specific probability distribution

ρ(λ|x,y) = [1 − λ1λ2(x·y)]/4.

Nor does it yet provide a dynamical or variational principle from which that distribution follows. That is one of the central unresolved problems for the proposed framework.

A.24 The Joint Distribution as the More Fundamental Object

The conventional measurement-independent picture begins with an underlying distribution

ρ(λ)

and then treats the measurement settings as independently selected variables.

The present proposal instead suggests that the more fundamental object may be a joint distribution over the complete set of relevant physical variables:

ρ(λ,x,y).

Measurement independence is precisely the factorization

ρ(λ,x,y)=ρ(λ)ρ(x,y).

The proposed framework allows the more general case

ρ(λ,x,y) ≠ ρ(λ)ρ(x,y).

Equivalently, provided the relevant probabilities are nonzero, one may express the dependence in either conditional direction:

P(λ|x,y) ≠ P(λ)

or

P(x,y|λ) ≠ P(x,y).

These are mathematically related descriptions of the same statistical dependence. Neither form, by itself, says which variable causally determines the other.

A.25 The Question of “Who Chose the Settings?”

The obvious objection is immediate: if λ is correlated with Alice's and Bob's settings, does that mean Alice and Bob were not free to choose their settings?

The answer requires care because the word free can refer to different concepts.

Bell's mathematical assumption is statistical independence. It says that the hidden variable and the measurement settings are statistically independent. It does not contain a complete philosophical theory of human freedom.

Conversely, rejecting statistical independence does not by itself specify a philosophical account of free will. It says only that the complete physical variables entering the experiment cannot be factorized in the way assumed by the Bell derivation.

This distinction will become the central subject of the next section. The fact that the mathematical proposal raises a question about free choice is not an accidental complication. It is the natural conceptual consequence of taking measurement dependence seriously.

A.26 Is the Correlation “Conspiratorial”?

A common objection to measurement-dependent models is that the required correlation appears extraordinarily coordinated. If the measurement settings are selected by complicated physical processes—perhaps involving random-number generators, human decisions, astronomical observations, or other distant events—why should the hidden variable associated with the source be correlated with those choices?

Calling the correlation “conspiratorial,” however, is already an interpretive judgment. The mathematical statement itself is simply

P(λ|x,y) ≠ P(λ).

Whether that dependence represents an implausible conspiracy, a global boundary condition, a retrocausal mechanism, a superdeterministic constraint, or some other physical principle cannot be determined from the equation alone.

The Block Universe perspective motivates a different possibility: perhaps the apparently independent events are not fundamentally independent because the complete physical history is selected as a globally consistent four-dimensional structure.

Whether such an account can be made physically natural rather than merely logically possible is an open question.

A.27 Relation to Retrocausal and Superdeterministic Terminology

The framework developed here lies close to ideas that are sometimes described in the foundations of physics literature as retrocausal, measurement-dependent, or superdeterministic. These labels overlap but are not identical.

A retrocausal model typically introduces a causal influence from a later boundary condition or measurement choice into an earlier physical variable. A superdeterministic model generally permits correlations between hidden variables and measurement settings such that the usual statistical independence assumption fails. A measurement-dependent model is the broader mathematical category in which

P(λ|a,b) ≠ P(λ).

The present manuscript does not need to commit at this stage to any one of these labels. Its central claim is narrower: the Block Universe makes it conceptually possible to regard measurement dependence as a property of a complete spacetime history rather than automatically as a signal traveling backward in time.

A.28 The Experimental Achievement Remains Intact

Nothing in this proposal changes the empirical status of Bell experiments.

The experiments still demonstrate correlations that violate Bell inequalities. Quantum mechanics still predicts those correlations. The explicit measurement-dependent model reproduces the singlet probabilities.

The proposed reinterpretation therefore does not dispute the experimental result. It changes the question asked of the result.

Instead of asking:

“Which nonlocal influence produces the Bell correlation?”

the proposal asks:

“Can the Bell correlation be a constraint on the joint structure of a complete local physical history?”

The second question is the one that leads directly from quantum foundations back to the Block Universe.

A.29 The Remaining Physical Questions

The explicit construction establishes an important mathematical possibility, but several major questions remain unanswered.

  • What physical principle determines the joint distribution? Why should complete histories be weighted in precisely the way required by quantum mechanics?

  • How is the distribution embedded in spacetime? A complete theory must specify what physical object or law carries the relevant correlations.

  • Can the full Born rule be recovered? Reproducing the singlet state is not enough to establish a general theory of quantum mechanics.

  • Can more complicated entangled states be reproduced? A viable theory must extend beyond the simplest two-particle example.

  • Can relativistic covariance be maintained? The proposal must not depend on an arbitrary preferred frame.

  • Can the apparent freedom of experimental choices be explained consistently? This is the conceptual problem that naturally leads into Section 5.

These questions prevent the present argument from being presented as a finished alternative to quantum mechanics. What has been established is a coherent route by which one of Bell's assumptions can be relaxed while preserving local response functions and reproducing the singlet statistics.

A.30 The Proposed Physical Ingredients

The framework developed in this section can now be summarized by its proposed ingredients:

  1. Four-dimensional spacetime described as a Block Universe.

  2. Local response functions A=A(x,λ) and B=B(y,λ).

  3. Quantum probabilities retained as genuine statistical structure.

  4. Hidden variables associated with individual physical histories.

  5. Measurement independence relaxed: ρ(λ|x,y) need not equal ρ(λ).

  6. Exact quantum correlations reproduced by the joint distribution.

  7. No operational superluminal signaling.

The central conceptual shift is from a picture in which each event is generated independently as time progresses to a picture in which the complete physical history is constrained as a whole.

A.31 From Local Dynamics to Global Consistency

This shift does not necessarily require abandoning local physics.

A local dynamical law specifies how physical quantities behave in a neighborhood of spacetime. A global consistency condition can additionally constrain which complete solutions of those local laws are physically realized.

The distinction can be expressed schematically:

Local dynamics → what relationships are permitted locally

Global consistency → which complete spacetime histories are realized

The proposal is that Bell correlations may belong partly to the second category. The local response of each measurement device remains local, while the complete history in which the device operates may be globally constrained.

This is precisely the kind of distinction that the Block Universe makes conceptually natural: the universe is not fundamentally a sequence of independent three-dimensional states being created one after another. It is a four-dimensional physical structure whose different regions must jointly satisfy the laws and boundary conditions governing the whole.
