5 The Block Universe and Free Will

What does a four-dimensional universe imply about human choice?

5.1 The question must begin with a definition of free will

The question of whether the Block Universe is compatible with free will cannot be answered without specifying what free will means. The term is used for several different claims: that people deliberate and act on reasons; that their actions can be attributed to them; that alternative outcomes may be physically possible; or, more strongly, that an agent possesses a special power to select which outcome becomes actual.

These claims should not be conflated. In particular, the existence of deliberation does not by itself establish a freedom that stands apart from the physical history. To keep the distinction visible, we will use three terms:

Deliberative will refers to the real physical processes through which a conscious observer evaluates alternatives, drawing on conscious and unconscious information processing, and arrives at a choice. The term does not imply any exemption from physical laws.

Perceived free will refers to the observer's subjective experience of having controlled which outcome occurred. The experience itself is real; whether its implied power of selection exists is a separate question.

Fundamental free will, in the strong sense examined here, refers to an additional power of an agent to determine which physically permissible outcome becomes actual, over and above the physical processes that constitute the agent and the realized history.

In discussing a specific choice between A and B, we will use decision for the point in the actual physical history at which A rather than B is established as the realized outcome. Prior intentions, reconsiderations, and apparent commitments are physical events too, but for this purpose they belong to the deliberative history leading to the outcome; they are not privileged as separate decisions. What counts as the outcome must, of course, be specified for the question under examination.

These definitions are analytical choices for the present discussion, not universal definitions used in philosophy or cognitive science. We will first review established conceptions of free will fairly, then explain the particular interpretation suggested by the physicalist Block Universe developed in this manuscript.

5.2 Physicalist free will

The first broad interpretation is that the conscious entity is entirely physical. Its deliberation is performed by physical processes, most plausibly processes occurring in the brain, and those processes obey the laws of physics.

Under this interpretation, there is no additional non-physical agent that has to intervene in the physical process in order for a choice to occur.

5.2.1 Physicalist-deterministic free will

In a deterministic physical theory, the complete physical state of the system together with the physical laws determines its subsequent physical states. A simplified representation is:

S(t0) + L → S(t1)

For a conscious entity, those states include the physical processes associated with perception, memory, reasoning, deliberation, preferences, intentions, decisions, and actions.

There is no conflict between this picture and the Block Universe. Indeed, the Block Universe is naturally capable of representing such a deterministic history. The successive states of the entity form part of its world line:

S1 → S2 → S3 → ···

The crucial point is that the Block Universe itself is indifferent to determinism. The Block Universe describes the complete four-dimensional history; it does not, by itself, specify whether the laws that generate or constrain that history are deterministic.

5.2.2 Physicalist-indeterministic free will

The same is true if the underlying physical laws contain an irreducibly random, or ontically random, component.

Instead of a physical state having only one possible successor, the laws might assign probabilities to several possible successors:

S(t0) + L → {S1, S1′, S1″, ···}

The Block Universe does not object to this. The actual four-dimensional universe simply contains the particular history that occurs.

This is consistent with the distinction developed in Section 4 between randomness and independence. A variable may be genuinely random and nevertheless have a definite value in the actual history.

The Block Universe specifies the actual history; it does not require the laws governing that history to be deterministic.

Thus, neither deterministic physicalism nor indeterministic physicalism is in conflict with the Block Universe merely by virtue of being deterministic or indeterministic.

5.3 Compatibilist free will

Compatibilism identifies free will with meaningful forms of agency exercised through a person's own reasons, values, preferences, and deliberation, rather than with a power to escape physical causation. On this account, an action may be free even when the processes producing it are entirely physical. Freedom may also depend on the absence of coercion or manipulation. Compatibilists therefore need not claim that an agent stands outside the laws of physics.

From the perspective adopted here, this is a defensible description of a genuine capability, but calling it free will risks confusing it with the stronger notion of an independent power to select an outcome. We prefer deliberative will for the physical capacity and perceived free will for the accompanying subjective experience. This is a terminological and interpretive preference, not a refutation of compatibilism.

The brain's organization, memories, preferences, conscious evaluation, and unconscious processing are all parts of one physical system. The detailed mechanism by which this system generates a subjective sense of agency is not fully understood. Nevertheless, it is reasonable within a physicalist account to treat that experience as emerging from the system, without claiming to have derived it from neuroscience.

The analogy with Section 2 is illuminating. Asymmetric physical records along an observer's world line help explain why time is experienced as flowing, even though the Block Universe requires no objectively advancing present. Likewise, the physical history contains representations of alternatives, deliberation, and the realized choice; an observer can experience this as exercising free will without that experience establishing an additional power outside the physical history.

experienced flow of time ↔ asymmetric physical records

perceived free will ↔ physical records of deliberation and realized choice

The analogy does not assert that the two experiences have identical neural mechanisms. Its point is ontological: a real subjective experience need not imply that the additional fundamental property attributed to it exists.

5.4 Libertarian free will

A stronger conception of free will holds that a person is genuinely capable of making alternative choices. In its strongest form, libertarian free will requires that the person be an ultimate source of the decision rather than merely the location at which a prior chain of physical causes terminates.

This raises the familiar question:

If a person's complete four-dimensional history contains deliberation followed by choice A, could that person have chosen B instead?

The answer depends on what is meant by could.

The Block Universe itself does not say that only one conceivable four-dimensional history exists. Consider two possible complete histories:

BA: Spre → A

BB: Spre → B

The two histories can be identical up to the decision event and differ only in the subsequent choice. Nothing about the Block Universe ontology itself prohibits such a pair of possible histories.

The Block Universe says that the actual universe is one complete four-dimensional history. It does not answer why the actual universe is BA rather than BB, nor does it by itself specify the physical laws or boundary conditions that determine which complete history is realized.

The Block Universe is not a theory of why this particular Block Universe exists rather than another possible Block Universe.

Consequently, the Block Universe should not be confused with determinism. Determinism is a claim about the dynamics of a physical theory. The Block Universe is an ontological description of spacetime.

Block Universe ≠ Deterministic Universe

Block Universe ≠ Predetermined Universe

A deterministic physical theory may imply that a particular prior state permits only one subsequent state. But that conclusion comes from the deterministic laws, not from the Block Universe concept itself.

Likewise, an indeterministic theory may permit multiple possible complete histories while the actual Block Universe contains only one of them.

5.5 Two meanings of “could have chosen differently”

The phrase “I could have chosen differently” therefore needs to be treated carefully. There are at least two distinct meanings.

5.5.1 Possible-history sense

There exists another physically possible complete history that is identical to the actual history up to the decision and in which the person chooses differently.

BA: Spre → A

BB: Spre → B

Under this interpretation, the Block Universe is entirely compatible with the statement that the person could have chosen differently.

5.5.2 Counterfactual-within-the-same-history sense

A stronger interpretation asks whether the person could have changed the actual outcome while leaving the actual four-dimensional history otherwise unchanged.

This is a substantially different proposition. The Block Universe does not require us to adopt it, and it is not necessary to establish that alternative complete histories are possible.

“Another possible history” is not the same thing as “another outcome within the same actual history.”

Once this distinction is made, the apparent contradiction between libertarian free will and the Block Universe becomes considerably less immediate.

5.6 Sourcehood and personhood

Another conception of free will focuses not on metaphysical indeterminacy but on sourcehood: whether the person is the genuine source of the action.

Every physical record in the universe has an antecedent history. The physical history of a human being can ultimately be traced backward through the history of life, the formation of the Earth, stellar and galactic evolution, and ultimately toward the earliest physical conditions of the universe.

Universe → galaxies → stars → planets → life → brains → deliberation → choice

But the existence of this larger causal history does not eliminate the person as a distinct physical and causal system.

A person's decision can simultaneously be described at different levels. It can be a physical event caused by the state of the person's brain, while also being an action caused by the person's beliefs, memories, preferences, intentions, and deliberation.

These descriptions need not compete with one another. The person is a physical structure within the larger causal structure of the universe.

In this sense, personhood and sourcehood can be understood as properties embodied along the person's world line. The fact that the world line has physical antecedents extending back through the universe does not erase the causal reality of the person at the scale at which the person's decisions and actions occur.

The existence of antecedent causes extending throughout the history of the universe does not eliminate the person as a distinct physical and causal system within that history.

At this point the question begins to move from physics into philosophy. Physics can describe the physical processes constituting the person and the causal relations among them. Whether that constitutes the kind of “ultimate authorship” required by a particular philosophical conception of free will is a further philosophical question.

5.7 Non-physical and dualist conceptions

A fundamentally different proposal is that conscious agency is not entirely physical. A soul, mind, or other non-physical agent could be proposed as an additional source of choice.

The Block Universe does not itself disprove such a proposal. The issue is different: without a theory describing the proposed non-physical entity and its interaction with the physical universe, the proposal does not provide a physical explanation of the choice.

For example, suppose we write:

brain state → non-physical agent chooses → brain state changes

We then need to ask what the state of the non-physical agent is, what rules govern its behavior, how it interacts with physical matter, and what observable consequences distinguish this account from a purely physical explanation.

Simply asserting that “the soul made the choice” relocates the unexplained mechanism rather than specifying it.

A non-physical theory could certainly be developed in principle, but it would require additional structure before it could function as an explanatory physical theory. Otherwise, sufficiently unconstrained metaphysical agency can be invoked to explain almost any outcome, making it difficult to distinguish the hypothesis empirically.

This limitation is not imposed by the Block Universe. It is a general requirement for constructing a testable physical explanation.

5.8 Free will and the independence of other conscious entities

Our original definition included another interesting condition: that an individual's deliberation should be independent of the internal deliberations of other conscious entities.

This should be distinguished from statistical correlation.

As established in Section 4, the existence of a correlation does not by itself imply a causal interaction. In particular:

correlation across spacetime ≠ interaction across spacetime

The same distinction applies to human decisions. If two people's decisions are correlated, that does not by itself establish that one person's deliberation has overridden or controlled the other's deliberation.

For free will, the more relevant question is whether another agent has bypassed or coerced the person's own deliberative process.

Thus statistical dependence should not automatically be identified with loss of agency. This is particularly important in the present framework, because the Block Universe permits correlations among events at different locations and times without requiring a corresponding physical signal propagating between them.

5.9 The Block Universe does not determine the origin of the actual history

We can now state an important limitation of the framework.

The Block Universe tells us that the actual universe is a complete four-dimensional history. It does not tell us why that particular history exists rather than another possible history.

Suppose two complete histories are physically possible:

BA = {all events, with choice A}

BB = {all events, with choice B}

The Block Universe framework does not contain an additional principle that says: “BA must exist rather than BB.”

That question belongs to whatever deeper theory specifies the physical laws, boundary conditions, probability structure, or other principles governing the universe.

This is analogous to the role of the Block Universe in our discussion of quantum mechanics. In Section 4, the Block Universe provided a framework in which complete four-dimensional histories could contain correlations without requiring instantaneous causal influence. It did not itself derive the particular quantum probability distribution

ρ(λ|x,y) = [1 − λ₁λ₂(x · y)] / 4.

Likewise, in the present discussion, the Block Universe provides the four-dimensional ontology but does not itself determine why a particular person's world line contains one choice rather than another.

5.10 A useful taxonomy of free will

Conception What makes an action free? Compatibility with the Block Universe
Physicalist-deterministic Physical deliberation produces the action under deterministic laws. Compatible. The Block Universe is indifferent to determinism.
Physicalist-indeterministic Physical deliberation produces the action while the underlying laws contain genuine indeterminacy. Compatible. The actual Block Universe contains one history permitted by the laws.
Compatibilist The action flows from the person's own reasons, memories, preferences, intentions, and deliberation. Compatible. The deliberative process is itself part of the person's world line.
Libertarian The person is genuinely capable of alternative choices and is an appropriate ultimate source of the decision. Not prohibited by the Block Universe. Alternative complete histories can be conceived without requiring the Block Universe to explain why the actual one is realized.
Sourcehood The action originates through the person's own deliberative and motivational structure. Compatible. A person remains a distinct causal system within the larger four-dimensional history.
Dualist / non-physical A non-physical component participates in or controls the decision. Not ruled out by the Block Universe, but requires an additional theory describing the non-physical agent and its interaction with the physical universe.

5.11 What the Block Universe actually implies about free will

Under the definition used throughout this manuscript, the actual Block Universe is the complete physical history of reality: one particular realization consistent with the laws of physics. It contains the observer, the brain, the deliberation, any genuinely random events, and the realized decision. It is not a stage on which an additional agent stands outside the history and selects what will happen.

This makes the phrase independent control problematic unless its meaning is specified. Independent of what? If it means independent of the complete physical history or able to suspend physical laws, it is incompatible with the physicalist Block Universe being considered here. If it refers instead to a physically lawful process inside the history, that process is already part of the universe; it may be described as agency or regulation, but it does not introduce a separate freedom to choose which complete history is actual.

Neither determinism nor indeterminism changes this reasoning. Deterministic laws may permit one outcome given a complete prior state; indeterministic laws may permit several with specified probabilities. Randomness does not confer control over the random result upon the agent. In either case the actual Block Universe contains the outcome that occurred.

This does not make deliberation unreal or causally irrelevant. The agent's reasoning is part of the physical account of the realized action. Nor does it imply that the action was externally coerced: a person and the physical processes constituting that person are not two competing sources of causation.

The observer is part of the realized history, not an additional selector of that history. Deliberative will is physically real; the stronger freedom commonly attributed to it is not established by the experience of choosing.

Here, to say that the laws of physics govern the decision means that the complete history must satisfy physical constraints, not that the laws themselves are conscious agents that actively choose outcomes. The Block Universe does not supply an explanation of why this permissible history exists rather than another.

5.12 The central conclusion

The distinction between deliberative will and fundamental free will allows us to state the present interpretation more precisely. An observer may consciously consider A and B, revise intentions, and ultimately realize A. Every part of that process is contained in the physical history. The decision, in the sense used here, is the realized A rather than B; earlier tentative commitments are simply events within the deliberation leading to it.

The observer may experience the process as an independent act of choosing. From the observer's immediate perspective, that experience may be indistinguishable from what the observer calls genuine free will. But the experience alone does not establish an additional power to select or revise the complete physical history.

This is parallel to our account of temporal flow. Physical records underpin an observer's experience of time passing without requiring an objectively moving present. Similarly, the physical processes and records of deliberation and choice can underpin an experience of free will without requiring a separate selector of physical reality.

Our preferred interpretation is that deliberative will is a real physical capability, while perceived free will is a real subjective experience. Neither entails fundamental freedom from the physical history or its governing laws.

The conclusion does not depend on determinism. It also does not purport to derive the subjective experience of agency from a completed theory of consciousness. It is an ontological interpretation grounded in the physicalist Block Universe assumed here, not a theorem of relativity alone or a demonstration that every philosophical account of agency is incoherent.

Alternative philosophical definitions, including compatibilist and sourcehood accounts, can remain meaningful descriptions of internal physical agency. What this interpretation rejects is the inference from those descriptions to an extra power of selection outside the complete physical history.

5.13 Transition to the next question

This leaves us with a more precise problem than the one with which we began.

How do physical processes within an observer produce the experience of choosing, and what can that experience legitimately tell us about reality?

That question takes us beyond the ontology of a complete four-dimensional history and toward the physical structure of observers, information, consciousness, and the relationship between deliberation and realized outcomes.

The same conceptual strategy that proved useful in the quantum-mechanical discussion can be applied here: distinguish the existence of a correlation or a complete history from the existence of an external causal influence.

global consistency ≠ external coercion

future existence ≠ absence of agency
