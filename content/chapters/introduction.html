Introduction

This manuscript grew out of a personal intellectual curiosity about physics and, more specifically, about the nature of physical reality. I am not a physicist, nor do I have the formal educational background necessary to develop or propose new physical theories. My academic and professional background is in engineering, which provides some useful analytical tools, but I approach the subjects discussed here as an amateur enthusiast seeking to understand questions that I find deeply interesting.

Of particular interest to me are the ontological questions underlying our descriptions of the physical universe. What does it mean for a physical event to exist? What is the nature of time? What does it mean for the universe to have a complete physical history? And how should we understand our own existence as conscious observers embedded within that reality?

Modern physics provides extraordinarily successful mathematical descriptions of the natural world. Yet a mathematical framework that accurately predicts observations does not necessarily provide an intuitively satisfying picture of what physical reality is. The distinction between predicting what we observe and understanding what those observations imply about the nature of reality is the principal motivation for this manuscript.

My objective is to develop a coherent way of thinking about several foundational topics in physics within a common ontological framework. Rather than treating questions about spacetime, the flow of time, quantum measurement, causality, and free will as entirely separate problems, I want to explore whether they can be understood from a small number of shared principles.

The guiding approach is to adopt as few additional postulates as possible, favoring those that appear logically reasonable and compatible with established physical laws. The hope is that a relatively simple ontological perspective can provide a more unified and intellectually satisfying interpretation of phenomena that otherwise appear disconnected or paradoxical.

The Block Universe interpretation of spacetime serves as the central organizing perspective for this exploration. Beginning with the implications of relativity, the manuscript examines how a four-dimensional description of physical reality might inform our understanding of time, physical laws, quantum mechanics, conscious experience, free will, and causal consistency.

The objective is interpretive rather than predictive. I do not claim to derive new physical laws, propose a replacement for existing theories, or produce new experimentally testable predictions. Nor do I suggest that ontological reasoning alone can establish the physical truth of a particular interpretation. The aim is more modest: to explore whether a coherent interpretation of physical reality can be constructed that remains consistent with what established physics tells us, while making as few additional assumptions as reasonably possible.

Many of the ideas discussed in this manuscript have been explored extensively by physicists and philosophers. I make no claim that the underlying concepts or arguments are original. What I hope to achieve is a synthesis that is personally meaningful: a connected set of arguments that I can revisit, question, and refine as my understanding develops.

The manuscript was developed through extensive interactive discussions with ChatGPT. I used these conversations to explore ideas, challenge assumptions, construct thought experiments, examine mathematical arguments, and fill gaps in my knowledge of physics. ChatGPT also assisted substantially in organizing and drafting the text. The resulting manuscript is therefore a collaborative product of my questions, reasoning, and interpretations, together with AI-assisted explanations, analysis, and writing. This assistance should not be mistaken for independent scientific validation, and any technical claims remain subject to verification against established literature.

Throughout this exploration, I have tried to distinguish between what established physics demonstrates, what a particular interpretation suggests, and what remains speculative or unresolved. Logical self-consistency is an essential requirement, but it is not sufficient to establish physical truth. Where the proposed framework cannot explain something, I would rather acknowledge that limitation than introduce unsupported assumptions simply to preserve an appealing picture.

Ultimately, this manuscript is written primarily for myself. It is a record of an ongoing attempt to understand the universe and our place within it. Its value to me lies not in whether it contributes something new to science, but in whether it provides a coherent intellectual framework that I can return to, reconsider, and improve.

If it also proves interesting or useful to another curious reader, that would be a welcome additional outcome.
