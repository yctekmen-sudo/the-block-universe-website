6 The Block Universe and Time Travel

Backward travel, causal interactions with the past, logical self-consistency, and closed timelike curves

The central issue raised by backward time travel is not whether a traveler’s worldline closes, but whether all events—including interactions with an earlier history—can belong to one logically consistent Block Universe.

6.1 Time Travel as a Question of Causal Structure

In special and general relativity, a massive observer follows a future-directed timelike worldline. Proper time increases along that worldline. Relativistic time dilation allows two observers to accumulate different amounts of proper time between meetings; this familiar effect is sometimes called travel into the future. The more challenging question is whether an observer can reach the chronological past of an event from which they departed.

Let A be the traveler’s departure event and B the arrival event. The chronological past I⁻(A) consists of events from which a future-directed timelike path to A exists. In flat spacetime this is the interior of A’s past light cone. Thus B ∈ I⁻(A) means that B can causally precede A by a timelike route. The causal past J⁻(A) also includes events connected to A by lightlike paths.

An arrival at a numerically smaller coordinate time is not enough to establish physical travel into the past. Coordinate labels can be changed. The invariant question is whether B lies in the causal or chronological past of A while the traveler reaches B by following a locally future-directed causal path from A. General relativity admits mathematical spacetimes with such nontrivial global causal relationships, although their physical realization remains uncertain.

Crucially, the traveler’s own worldline need not close. The observer can depart at A, arrive at B, and continue onward without ever returning to the departure event. Backward time travel and a closed traveler worldline are therefore different concepts.

6.2 Open Worldlines and Encounters with the Earlier History

After arriving at B in the chronological past of A, the traveler can follow an open worldline. Several possibilities arise. The traveler might avoid any interaction with their younger self. They might send a message to their younger self. Or they might interact with other people and objects whose subsequent histories affect the circumstances of departure at A.

A meeting between older and younger versions of the same person does not require the older traveler to revisit the departure event A. The two versions can be present in the same region of spacetime at an event distinct from A, or communicate across distinct events. If they literally occupy the same event, the traveler’s full worldline has a self-intersection there; it need not be a globally periodic worldline. A message exchanged across separate events can leave the material worldline non-self-intersecting.

The relevant condition is that the earlier person’s memories and records, the later departure, and the older traveler’s actions all agree as parts of one physical history. If the younger observer remembers receiving a warning, then the older observer who eventually sends it cannot belong to an otherwise identical history in which the warning was never received.

Even when there is no direct encounter, indirect causal effects may matter. Avoiding one’s younger self removes an obvious opportunity for contradiction, but it does not independently prove the complete history consistent. Conversely, interaction with one’s younger self is not itself paradoxical.

6.3 The Grandfather Paradox Without a Closed Traveler Worldline

The grandfather paradox is an example of backward time travel with a potentially open traveler worldline. A traveler departs at A, arrives at B in an earlier era, and attempts to kill an ancestor before the ancestor has the descendants required for the traveler’s own birth. The traveler need not return to A, and the observer’s material trajectory need not form a closed loop.

The contradiction arises from the stipulated facts: the traveler exists and makes the journey, yet the intervention is specified to prevent the conditions necessary for that same traveler to exist. Both claims cannot be true of one complete history under the assumed genealogy and circumstances.

The paradox is often narrated as though a previously completed past were subsequently modified. In the Block Universe developed in Section 1, however, departure, arrival, intervention, and their consequences are all events in one four-dimensional history. There is no second temporal process in which an already existing past is rewritten.

This shows why the grandfather paradox is more general than a paradox involving a traveler who returns to the same spacetime event. Its essential feature is a contradictory causal specification, not geometric closure of the observer’s worldline.

6.4 Logical Self-Consistency as a Condition for Existence

The preceding examples motivate a principle broader than time travel: logical self-consistency is a necessary condition for physical existence. A complete history containing mutually contradictory facts cannot be an actual physical history.

Exists(H) ⇒ LogicallyConsistent(H).

The implication is one-way. Logical consistency does not establish that a hypothetical history exists, nor that it satisfies Einstein’s field equations, quantum theory, conservation laws, or appropriate boundary conditions. A consistent imagined history may still be physically impossible.

Applied to the grandfather paradox, this principle does not posit a special force that jams the weapon or arranges a last-minute rescue. It states only that the contradictory set of events cannot constitute one actual history. A specific consistent history may contain a failed attempt, different ancestry, or some other outcome, but the principle by itself does not predict which outcome occurs.

The same requirement applies whether the traveler’s worldline is open or closed, and whether causal contact with the younger self is direct, indirect, or absent. Time travel makes the constraint conspicuous; it does not create the requirement.

6.5 Permitted Causal Paths Versus Realized Physical Histories

General relativity describes a spacetime metric and the causal paths it permits. The actual Block Universe additionally contains the matter, radiation, observers, interactions, and physical records that are realized. A geometrically permitted trajectory that no object follows is not an extra material history or a second copy of an observer.

If the traveler goes from A to B along a future-directed timelike route while B lies in I⁻(A), there must also be a future-directed timelike route from B to A. Concatenating these routes establishes that the geometry admits a closed timelike path. This conclusion does not require the traveler to take the return route.

Thus an open actual traveler worldline can coexist with closed timelike curves admitted by the spacetime geometry. The existence of a geometric curve is not the same claim as the existence of a physical observer traversing it. The distinction matters when interpreting general relativity, but the ontological analysis of paradoxes concerns the actual events and their consistency.

Where an actual traveler affects their own earlier history, the relevant causal relationships can constrain that history without requiring the traveler’s own worldline to close.

6.6 Closed Timelike Curves as a Special Geometrical Case

A closed timelike curve (CTC) is a future-directed timelike curve that returns to the same spacetime event. Writing the curve as γ(τ), with τ proper time, its geometric closure can be represented by

γ(τ + T) = γ(τ), T > 0.

Proper time increases locally throughout the circuit. The curve is globally closed even though the observer never reverses their own local temporal direction or exceeds the speed of light.

Gödel’s rotating-universe solution provides a classic mathematical example. Certain idealized Kerr extensions and hypothetical time-shifted wormhole constructions also motivate discussions of chronology violation. These geometries are not evidence that realistic time machines exist.

The conceptual example A → B → A can be read in two ways. If the traveler actually takes both future-directed segments, the full path is closed. If the traveler takes only A → B and proceeds elsewhere, their path remains open, although a geometrically permitted B → A route may still exist. In neither case should a merely permitted return route be mistaken for an additional physical traveler.

6.7 A Closed Worldline and the Traveler’s Physical State

Geometric closure identifies the same spacetime event after a circuit, but a physical traveler also has internal degrees of freedom: a clock reading, memories, biological state, and interactions with the environment. These cannot be assigned arbitrarily to different traversals of the same event.

For a single-valued physical state S transported around a genuinely periodic worldline, consistency requires

S(τ + T) = S(τ).

Equivalently, if F_T represents the state evolution around a circuit, a consistent periodic state is a fixed point: F_T(S) = S. A given physical evolution law need not possess such a state. Geometry alone does not guarantee a realizable material traveler.

This explains why an ordinary-looking B → A journey can seem to create two copies at A. In the idealized smooth periodic case, the returning and departing portions are the same locally identified history, not two independently specified bodies that must occupy the same event with conflicting states. A self-intersecting but nonperiodic trajectory, or a model with multiple streams meeting, requires a more detailed matter description; geometric coincidence alone does not identify all internal states.

The state-matching condition is therefore a strong constraint on a realized closed physical trajectory. It is not a requirement that every open-worldline traveler returning to an earlier era become a periodic physical system.

6.8 Proper Time, Memory, and the Experienced Direction of Time

Section 2 explained the experienced direction of time in terms of asymmetric physical records. An observer remembers events represented in earlier physical states but does not ordinarily possess corresponding records of later states. This information asymmetry is linked to thermodynamic conditions, rather than to an external movement of a universal present.

An observer on an open backward-time journey continues to experience increasing proper time. The same local statement applies along a CTC. In either case, a journey to the chronological past of departure need not feel like time running backward.

Whether an observer could detect the global closure of their own worldline is a separate question. Some spacetime configurations may offer observable evidence of unusual causal structure; others may not. The mere subjective experience of a succession of remembered states does not establish that a worldline is globally open.

One must not assume that memory information strictly increases at every point along every worldline. Forgetting, erasure, equilibration, and exchange with the environment are physical possibilities. Nonetheless, the requirement of a globally consistent state around a realized periodic curve can be highly restrictive for an ordinary memory-forming organism.

6.9 Thermodynamics and Recurrent Physical States

Ordinary biological activity and memory formation typically involve dissipative processes. If a physically isolated system is required to return to precisely the same state after one circuit, then a strictly increasing single-valued entropy around that circuit is incompatible with closure.

S_entropy(τ + T) = S_entropy(τ),

whereas

dS_entropy/dτ > 0

everywhere would imply a contradiction.

This is a conditional incompatibility, not a general theorem excluding every CTC. Thermodynamic entropy increase is statistical; local decreases and transfers of entropy between subsystems and environments must be treated within the full physical model. Nor is there necessarily a globally defined time coordinate or universal entropy function that increases on every causal trajectory in a chronology-violating spacetime.

A closed parameterized curve may be traversed repeatedly in its mathematical description: γ(τ + nT) = γ(τ). These repeated parameter values do not, by themselves, create infinitely many new spacetime events, independent copies of the traveler, or repeated deposits of heat. Distinct matter streams or physical interactions, if present in a proposed model, must still be accounted for.

The appropriate conclusion is modest: thermodynamics places additional constraints on physically realizable closed trajectories, particularly those involving ordinary observers, but it does not by itself convert every proposed time-travel history into a logical contradiction.

6.10 Global Consistency Without a Dynamical Intervention

Section 3 described physical laws as global relationships satisfied by a complete four-dimensional history, rather than necessarily as step-by-step instructions executed by an external clock. This viewpoint makes the time-travel puzzle less mysterious.

If the actual Block Universe contains an older traveler sending a message to a younger self, the reception, the later memories, the departure, and the transmission are all parts of one history. Nothing is added to a previously finished past. A contradictory description is not a real history awaiting repair.

This resembles the interpretation proposed in Section 4 for globally correlated quantum measurement settings and outcomes. In that discussion, global correlations need not be generated by superluminal signals or by a mechanism anticipating future choices. Here, consistent relationships between earlier and later events similarly need not be produced by a universe that detects paradoxes and intervenes to prevent them.

The analogy is conceptual, not a derivation. The Block Universe does not calculate which consistent time-travel history occurs, establish the dynamics of a time machine, or derive the statistical predictions of quantum mechanics. It offers an ontological interpretation of histories that are otherwise physically admissible.

6.11 Physical Possibility and the Central Conclusion

General relativity admits mathematical spacetimes with nontrivial causal structure, including closed timelike curves. Whether realistic matter and quantum effects permit the formation or traversal of such regions remains unresolved. The logical self-consistency principle neither proves time travel possible nor substitutes for the dynamical laws that any proposed geometry and matter configuration must obey.

The grandfather paradox illustrates the central point without requiring the traveler’s own worldline to close. Backward time travel may create causal relationships with an observer’s earlier history, and those relationships must form one consistent set of physical facts. The traveler may interact with a younger self, influence earlier events indirectly, or avoid such interactions; none of these alternatives escapes the general requirement of logical consistency.

Closed timelike curves are a special geometric manifestation of chronology violation. A spacetime can admit them without any observer actually traversing one, while an observer who does follow a genuinely closed physical worldline faces additional state-matching and thermodynamic constraints.

The Block Universe does not portray time travel as rewriting the past. It portrays any physically realized journey, its causes, its interactions, and its consequences as parts of a single four-dimensional history. Logical consistency is necessary for that history to exist; physical laws determine which consistent histories, if any, are realizable.
